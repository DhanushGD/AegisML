# 1. What Does Stage 5 Actually Do?
Right now, your model is a snapshot in time—it knows what language looked like when the UC Berkeley dataset was collected.
Stage 5 (Evidently AI) acts as a smoke detector. It reads two datasets:
The Reference Data: Your original validation dataset (data/processed/val.csv).
The Current Data: The live user inputs saved inside your data/production_logs.db.

Evidently AI calculates statistical properties (like text length, vocabulary distributions, or embedding shifts) and answers the question: “Is the language my users are typing today statistically different from the language my model was trained on?”

# 2. The Retraining Query: With What Data Do We Retrain?
This is a premier system design interview question. You do not retrain using the continuous probabilities generated by your model. (If you do, the model just amplifies its own biases and mistakes—a failure mode called Model Collapse).

Instead, you retrain using a blended combination of both old and brand-new data:
[Original Training Data (v1)] ──┐
                                ├──► [Merged Dataset (v2)] ──► [Retrained Model]
[Labeled Production Logs] ──────┘
When Evidently AI triggers a drift alert, here is the automated loop that occurs:
1. Data Export: You extract the drifted texts from your SQLite production_logs.db.
2. The Labeling Hand (The Key Ingredient): Since these live comments don't have ground-truth answers, they are sent to a human annotation queue (or a larger, slower LLM like GPT-4) to get explicit, true labels ($0$ or $1$) matching your exact structure: label_hate_speech, label_insult, etc.
3. Data Blending: You append these newly labeled production examples to your original training dataset. This forces the model to retain its foundational knowledge while explicitly adapting to the new slang or topics it previously missed.

The Challenge: Your model evaluates text like "yo this update is actually fire ngl" and scores it completely wrong because it has never seen the word "fire" used as an adjective.
The Isolation: Evidently identifies that the word composition distribution has drifted heavily.
The Extraction: Your orchestration system pulls those specific flagged inputs out of your SQLite storage: SELECT input_text FROM inference_logs WHERE...
The Ground Truth Hand: You send those drifted phrases to a labeling task (human reviewers or an LLM ensemble). They add the true targets: label_insult=0, label_violence=0, etc.
The Retrain Phase: You combine your original Berkeley training rows with these newly labeled real-world logs to form Dataset V2. You train your model on this blended pool.

This introduces the true value of your work. The model maintains its deep understanding of toxicity but learns the latest context and slang, preventing accuracy degradation over time.
